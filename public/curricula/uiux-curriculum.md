# Learn UI/UX Design

> From first wireframe to systems and flows people can actually use

A three-level curriculum for UI/UX Designers. Learn visual fundamentals and Figma, then design systems and interaction, then ship with product and engineering — accessible, consistent interfaces with a documented process, not just pretty screens.

## Prerequisites

- Comfort using a computer and learning a professional design tool
- Willingness to show work in progress and take critique
- A product you use often enough to notice what is confusing

## Tools & Technologies

- Figma
- FigJam / Miro
- Design systems
- Figma prototyping
- Contrast checkers
- Whimsical
- Notion
- Material Design / Apple HIG

## Visual Design & Figma Foundations — Beginner

**Goal:** Comfortable designing clear static screens and simple flows in Figma
**Duration:** 4–6 wks

### Visual Design Principles

#### Hierarchy, type & color

- Type scale, weight, and line length for UI
- Color: brand, semantic (success/error), and contrast
- Spacing, alignment, and grouping so screens scan

#### Layout & composition

- Grids, margins, and consistent spacing systems
- Mobile-first vs. desktop: density and thumb zones
- Cards vs. lists vs. tables — pick the pattern for the job

### Figma Fundamentals

#### Frames, auto-layout & components

- Frames vs. groups; constraints for resize
- Auto-layout for rows, stacks, and padding
- Components, variants, and when to detach

#### From wireframe to mock

- Low-fidelity first: boxes and labels before polish
- Hi-fi: applying type, color, and real copy
- Naming layers and pages so a teammate can open the file

### Flows & Product Patterns

#### Common screens

- Onboarding, login, home, list, detail, and forms
- Empty, loading, and error states as first-class screens
- Navigation: tabs, stacks, and when a modal is wrong

#### Platform conventions

- Material and iOS HIG as starting points, not cages
- Mobile vs. web: tap targets, hover, and keyboards
- Copy in the UI: labels, buttons, and error messages

### Project Work

- Redesign one cluttered screen using only type, spacing, and hierarchy (no new features)
- Low-fi wireframes for a 4-screen flow (e.g. SACCO deposit or send-money)
- High-fidelity login + home for a small product, with empty and error states
- Type and color scale plus a short rationale for a brand

## Systems, Interaction & Prototypes — Intermediate

**Goal:** Build reusable components, interactive prototypes, and flows you can test with people
**Duration:** 6–8 wks

### Design Systems

#### Tokens & components

- Color, type, radius, and spacing as tokens
- Buttons, inputs, lists, and dialogs as a library
- Variants: size, state, and intent — without exploding the file

#### Using and extending a system

- When to reuse vs. invent a new pattern
- Documentation in Figma: usage, do/don't
- Working from an existing system (Material, a company kit)

### Interaction Design

#### States & feedback

- Hover, focus, pressed, disabled, and loading
- Validation: inline vs. on submit
- Destructive actions: confirm, undo, and copy

#### Complex flows

- Multi-step forms and progress
- Permissions, payments, and KYC-style friction
- Responsive: same feature on phone and desktop

### Prototyping & Critique

#### Prototypes for testing

- Figma prototypes: flows, overlays, and back stacks
- Fidelity: when a clickable wireframe is enough
- Task-based prototypes vs. marketing demos

#### Critique & iteration

- Giving and receiving critique without defending pixels
- Annotating decisions: problem, options, choice
- Version history and presenting a before/after

### Project Work

- Small component library: buttons, text fields, lists, and alerts with states
- Interactive prototype of a multi-step flow (onboarding, checkout, or loan application)
- Five-task usability test on the prototype and a short findings list
- Responsive web + mobile of the same feature, sharing one component set

## Product Design at Scale — Expert

**Goal:** Ship accessible, documented design that engineering can build and product can measure
**Duration:** 8–10 wks

### Accessibility & Quality

#### Accessible visual design

- WCAG contrast, focus order, and tap targets
- Color not as the only signal; motion and reduced-motion
- Forms, errors, and labels that work with assistive tech

#### Design QA

- Handoff: specs, redlines, and asset export
- Checking the build against the file
- Edge cases engineering will hit first if you skip them

### Working With Product & Engineering

#### The design brief and the PRD

- Translating a PM problem into flows and options
- Presenting 2–3 directions with a recommendation
- Scope cuts that preserve the user job

#### Handoff that does not stall the sprint

- Dev-ready files: components, naming, and inspect
- Copy, empty states, and error strings in the source of truth
- Design tickets vs. sitting in the engineering stand-up

### Systems & Craft at Scale

#### Design ops

- Governance: who can change the system
- Contribution model for new components
- Audit of a live product vs. the kit

#### Judgment

- When research is required vs. a heuristic pass
- Visual polish vs. shipping the job-to-be-done
- Ethics: dark patterns, dark patterns adjacent, and saying no

### Project Work

- End-to-end feature: research notes, system components, prototype, and handoff file
- Accessibility pass on a flow with annotated specs and a contrast report
- Design a complex flow (KYC, payments, or role-based permissions) including failure paths
- Critique a live product and propose a measured redesign with before/after and rationale
