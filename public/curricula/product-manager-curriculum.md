# Learn Product Management

> From problem briefs to shipped outcomes — decide what to build and why

A three-level curriculum for Product Managers. Learn to frame problems, write requirements teams can build from, run discovery with design and engineering, and own outcomes with roadmaps, metrics, and experiments — not a backlog of features.

## Prerequisites

- Comfort writing clearly for other people (emails, briefs, or docs)
- Willingness to talk to users and sit with incomplete information
- Basic numeracy: percentages, trends, and reading a simple chart

## Product Foundations — Beginner

**Goal:** Frame a problem, write requirements a team can build from, and prioritize a first backlog
**Duration:** 4–6 wks

### What Product Managers Do

#### The PM role

- Discovery vs. delivery — and why both are the job
- PM vs. project manager vs. founder vs. designer
- What you own: problem, outcome, and communication — not the code

#### How product teams work

- The trio: product, design, and engineering
- Stakeholders: sales, support, ops, and leadership
- A feature's path from idea to release

### Problems, Users & Evidence

#### Problem statements

- Who hurts, how often, and what they do today
- Problem vs. solution: keep the brief solution-agnostic
- Opportunity size without fake TAM slides

#### Lightweight discovery

- Five customer conversations beat a survey of 200 strangers
- Jobs to be done and the job-to-be-done interview
- Shadowing support tickets and sales calls

#### Competitive & market awareness

- Teardown: flows, pricing, and what they optimize for
- Substitutes (including spreadsheets and WhatsApp groups)
- When to ignore competitors and when not to

### Requirements & Prioritization

#### User stories & acceptance criteria

- As a / I want / so that — and when that template fails
- Acceptance criteria that QA and engineering can test
- Edge cases: empty, error, permission, and offline

#### Specs teams can use

- One-pager vs. PRD vs. ticket — pick the lightest that works
- Scope: in, out, and later
- Open questions and who will answer them

#### Prioritization

- Impact vs. effort, RICE, and MoSCoW
- Must-haves vs. nice-to-haves for a first release
- Saying no with a reason, not a delay

### Project Work

- Problem brief for a familiar product (mobile money, SACCO member app, or campus marketplace)
- User stories and acceptance criteria for a small feature (e.g. bill-split or deposit confirmation)
- Prioritized backlog of 10 items with a one-line rationale each
- Competitive teardown of two similar products: flows, pricing, and what you would steal

## Shipping With Teams — Intermediate

**Goal:** Run discovery-to-delivery with design and engineering, and communicate a roadmap with trade-offs
**Duration:** 6–8 wks

### Agile & the Delivery Cadence

#### Ceremonies that earn their time

- Sprint planning, standup, review, and retro — what each is for
- Story points, capacity, and when estimates are theater
- Definition of ready and definition of done

#### Unblocking the team

- Cutting scope mid-sprint without wrecking trust
- Dependencies, blockers, and who owns the chase
- Bugs vs. features: a simple triage rule

### Specs, Scope & Handoff

#### Working with design

- Briefing a designer: problem, constraints, and success
- Commenting in Figma without designing in the margins
- Usability findings that change the spec

#### Working with engineering

- Technical constraints as product input, not a veto
- API contracts, feature flags, and rollout plans
- Estimates, spikes, and when to spike vs. build

#### Release communication

- Release notes for users vs. internal changelog
- Go / no-go: quality, support readiness, and known issues
- Rollback and what you tell customers if it fails

### Metrics in the Loop

#### Product analytics basics

- Activation, retention, and a North Star that is not vanity
- Event naming and funnels you can actually trust
- Reading a dashboard without overfitting last week's dip

#### Experiments without overclaiming

- Hypothesis, metric, and guardrail before you ship a test
- Sample size and why a 3-day test is usually noise
- When a qualitative study beats an A/B test

### Stakeholders & Roadmaps

#### Roadmaps that survive contact

- Now / next / later vs. dated Gantt fiction
- Themes and outcomes, not a list of tickets
- Communicating trade-offs to leadership

#### Stakeholder management

- Sales and support as discovery channels
- Escalations: what is a bug, a request, and a strategy debate
- Written updates that replace status meetings

### Project Work

- Full PRD for a new feature (problem, users, scope, AC, analytics, risks)
- Two-week sprint plan plus retro notes for a mock team
- Now/next/later roadmap slide with three explicit trade-offs for leadership
- Spec a KYC, checkout, or M-Pesa collection flow including empty, error, and retry states

## Strategy, Outcomes & Influence — Expert

**Goal:** Own outcomes, not output — strategy, experiments, and decisions that survive scrutiny
**Duration:** 8–10 wks

### Product Strategy

#### Strategy that is not a slogan

- Where to play, how to win, and what you will not do
- Connecting company goals to a product area
- Kill criteria: when a bet is over

#### Portfolio & sequencing

- Bets vs. hygiene vs. keep-the-lights-on
- Platform work vs. customer-facing work
- Sequencing: what must be true before the next bet

### Growth, Retention & Decision Quality

#### Loops and funnels

- Acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue (when each applies)
- Cohorts and why last-week totals lie
- Pricing, packaging, and willingness to pay at a working level

#### Experiment systems

- Experiment briefs, holdouts, and peeking
- Feature flags as product, not only as engineering
- Post-ship reviews: what we learned, not just what we launched

### Influence & Ethics

#### Decision-grade communication

- Options, recommendation, and what you would need to change your mind
- Executive vs. operator audiences
- Writing that survives a 15-minute read

#### Ethics & constraints

- Dark patterns, consent, and data you should not collect
- Accessibility and inclusion as product requirements
- Regulatory and payments constraints (KYC, AML, consumer protection) as design input

### Project Work

- Strategy memo plus a two-quarter roadmap with kill criteria
- Experiment design for an activation or retention bet (hypothesis, metric, sample, guardrails)
- KPI tree and dashboard brief for a product area (e.g. collections, active members, or onboarding)
- Stakeholder readout with a recommendation, alternatives, and limitations
